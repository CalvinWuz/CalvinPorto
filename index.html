How I think

Principles that
outlast the tools.

01

Start with the problem

The best solution is usually the simplest one that genuinely fits. I reach for tools last, not first.

02

Design for the human

Technology should bend toward people, not the other way around. I keep the person on the other side of the screen in frame the whole time.

03

Stay curious on purpose

Every project is a chance to learn something I didn't know yesterday. Curiosity is my default state, not a phase.

04

Build security in, early

Security isn't a feature you bolt on at the end. It's a decision you make at the start, in every layer.

05

Sweat the quiet details

The details no one notices are the ones that make an experience feel effortless. I sweat them on purpose.

Selected thoughts

A few things I
keep coming back to.

01

What security taught me about trust

Studying how systems fail taught me that trust isn't a feature you ship. It's the sum of every small promise you keep. The best security is invisible; people only notice it when it's missing. So I stopped thinking about protection and started thinking about the quiet confidence a good product gives the person using it.

02

Why details matter

A four-pixel misalignment won't crash anything. But the user feels it, even if they can't name it. Details are where respect for the people using your work becomes visible. Craft is just empathy that someone bothered to act on, over and over, in the places no one will thank you for.

03

Building before feeling ready

I've never felt fully ready to start. I've learned to start anyway, because clarity comes from building, not from waiting for it. The first version is always a little wrong, and that's exactly the point: it shows you what the right version wants to be.
